Cell balancing is how a BMS keeps all the cells in a battery pack at similar state of charge (SoC).
Since cells never age or behave exactly the same, balancing is critical for safety, capacity, and lifespan—especially in lithium-ion packs.



Why balancing matters

Prevents overcharge/over-discharge of individual cells

Maximizes usable capacity (the weakest cell otherwise limits the pack)

Extends battery life and improves reliability




1) Passive balancing (most common)

What it does: Bleeds extra charge from higher-voltage cells through resistors

When: Usually near the top of charge

Pros: Simple, cheap, reliable

Cons: Wastes energy as heat, slower

Common in consumer electronics and many EV packs where cost and simplicity matter.



2) Active balancing

What it does: Transfers energy from high cells to low cells (via capacitors, inductors, or DC-DC converters)

When: Can operate during charge, discharge, or rest

Pros: Efficient, faster, preserves energy

Cons: More complex and expensive

Seen in high-end EVs, large storage systems, and performance applications.

When balancing happens

Top balancing: Equalizes cells at full charge (common with passive methods)

Bottom balancing: Equalizes near empty (less common, trickier)

Continuous/dynamic: Active systems can balance anytime





What a BMS actually controls

Cell voltage sensing

Balance enable/disable per cell

Safety cutoffs (OV/UV, over-temp, over-current)

Reporting SoC/SoH to the system
